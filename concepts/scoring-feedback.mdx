Every answer you submit in Viva Mate AI comes back with a score and a detailed feedback report. This is not just a number — it is a diagnostic tool. The score tells you how well your answer performed against exam standards, and the feedback tells you exactly what to do next. Learning to read and act on your scores is one of the fastest ways to improve your viva performance.

## How Scoring Works

Answers are scored on a scale of **0 to 10**. The score reflects three dimensions of your response, each weighted in the overall assessment:

- **Concept coverage.** Did your answer include the key ideas, terms, and relationships that a strong response to this question requires? This is the largest component of your score. An answer can be fluently written but still score low if it omits essential concepts.
- **Accuracy.** Were the concepts you included correct? Partially correct statements, misconceptions, or technically imprecise claims reduce your score in this dimension.
- **Clarity.** Was your answer organised and easy to follow? In a real viva, examiners credit responses that are well-structured and clearly articulated. Vague or meandering answers — even when they contain correct ideas — score lower than precise, well-ordered ones.

The three dimensions combine into a single score that reflects the overall quality of your answer as a viva response.

## Score Bands

| Score | Band | What It Means |
|-------|------|---------------|
| 8–10 | **Excellent** | Strong answer — key concepts are covered accurately and clearly. You demonstrate a confident, well-organised understanding of the topic. Minor omissions may exist but do not materially weaken the response. |
| 5–7 | **Good** | Partially correct answer — you have identified some of the important concepts but missed others, or your explanation lacks sufficient precision or depth in places. A solid foundation that needs targeted reinforcement. |
| 2–4 | **Developing** | Key concepts are missing or significantly underdeveloped. Your answer addresses the question at a surface level but does not demonstrate the depth of understanding expected in a viva. Use the model answer to identify and close specific gaps. |
| 0–1 | **Needs Work** | The answer does not substantively address the question. This may indicate a gap in foundational knowledge for this topic. Review the relevant material before attempting the question again. |

## Reading Your Feedback

After every answer, your feedback report is divided into three sections. Take time to read each one — not just the score.

<Accordion title="Score Summary">
  The Score Summary shows your numerical score and band label alongside a one-sentence rationale for the rating. This gives you a concise overall assessment before you read the detail. Use it as a headline, not the full picture.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Gaps Identified">
  This section lists the specific concepts or ideas that were missing or underdeveloped in your answer. Each gap is named and briefly explained — so you understand not just what was missing, but why it matters for this question. If a gap appears repeatedly across multiple questions, it signals a topic area that needs focused revision.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Model Answer">
  The model answer is a reference-quality response written to viva exam standard. It is not a word-for-word script to memorise — it is a benchmark that shows you what a strong answer looks like in terms of content, structure, and level of detail. Compare it to your own answer carefully: notice what it includes that yours did not, and how the ideas are sequenced and expressed.
</Accordion>

<Note>
  Scores are private and only visible to you.
</Note>

## Using Feedback to Improve

Reading your feedback is only the first step. Acting on it is what drives improvement. Use these strategies to turn your feedback into better performance.

- **Re-attempt the same question.** After reviewing the model answer, close it and try the question again from scratch. The second attempt forces you to reconstruct the answer using your own understanding rather than copying phrasing — which is exactly the skill a viva tests.
- **Target your lowest-scoring topics.** Your Progress tab tracks your average score by topic over time. Look for topics where your average is consistently in the Developing or Needs Work band — these are your highest-priority areas before exam day.
- **Treat gaps as a revision list.** Every gap identified in your feedback corresponds to a specific concept. Collect recurring gaps across sessions and use them to build a focused revision plan, rather than re-reading entire topic notes from scratch.
- **Track improvement across attempts.** If you attempt the same question multiple times over different sessions, compare your scores. Consistent improvement on previously low-scoring questions is a strong signal that your revision is working.
- **Use the model answers as writing models.** Strong viva answers have a recognisable structure: define the concept, explain the mechanism, give an example or application, note a limitation or trade-off. Study how model answers are built and practise applying the same pattern to new questions.
