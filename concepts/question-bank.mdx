## Question Types

Viva Mate AI includes four distinct question types, each designed to test a different dimension of your knowledge.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Conceptual">
    **Define or explain a term, process, or principle.**

    These questions test whether you understand the core ideas behind a topic — not just that you have heard the term, but that you can explain it clearly and accurately.

    _Examples:_
    - "What is regularisation, and why is it used in machine learning models?"
    - "Explain the difference between supervised and unsupervised learning."
    - "What does the Central Limit Theorem state?"
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Applied">
    **Calculate, compare, or justify a choice between approaches.**

    These questions push you beyond definition into practical reasoning. You need to demonstrate that you can use your knowledge, not just recall it.

    _Examples:_
    - "Compare L1 and L2 regularisation. When would you prefer one over the other?"
    - "A model has high training accuracy but low validation accuracy. What does this indicate, and how would you address it?"
    - "Calculate the entropy of a dataset with three equally probable classes."
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Scenario-Based">
    **Given a dataset or real-world situation, describe what you would do.**

    These questions simulate the kind of open-ended problem-solving an examiner might test in a project viva or applied methods examination.

    _Examples:_
    - "You are given a tabular dataset with 40% missing values. Walk me through your approach to handling this before modelling."
    - "Your classifier achieves 95% accuracy on a highly imbalanced dataset. Is this a good result? What would you do next?"
    - "Describe how you would choose and validate a clustering algorithm for an unlabelled dataset."
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Code-Related">
    **Explain the logic, output, or design of an algorithm or code snippet.**

    These questions ask you to reason about implementation — understanding what a piece of code does, why it is structured that way, and what its limitations are.

    _Examples:_
    - "Explain what backpropagation does and how it updates weights in a neural network."
    - "What does this line of code do, and what would happen if you changed the learning rate to 1.0?"
    - "Describe the time complexity of the k-nearest neighbours algorithm at prediction time."
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Difficulty Levels

Every question in the bank is assigned one of three difficulty levels. These levels correspond to the depth of knowledge and the complexity of reasoning required to answer well.

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Beginner** | Tests foundational definitions and core concepts. Suitable for early revision or students new to a topic. Answers typically require accurate recall and basic explanation. |
| **Intermediate** | Requires you to connect concepts, compare approaches, or apply knowledge to a given context. Suitable for students with a working understanding of the subject. |
| **Advanced** | Demands deep reasoning, nuanced judgment, and the ability to discuss trade-offs, limitations, and edge cases. Reflects the level of questioning expected in a postgraduate viva or project defence. |

<Tip>
  Start with Beginner questions to build confidence, then work up to Advanced.
</Tip>
